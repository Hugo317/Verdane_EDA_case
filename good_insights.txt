COMPANY GROWTH
    Problem: no prior context on the company's growth rate, seasonality, etc.
    Plan: check YoY growth rate and seasonality.
    Data: overall growth, profitability and customer acquisition.
    Analysis: growth over the past 2 years is ~3x YoY, which is strong.
    There is a break in the last 2 quarters where growth slows (possibly COVID).
    Customer count is growing and baskets are getting bigger.
    No clear seasonality.
    Acquired customers are ordering more often over time and staying loyal.
    Conclusion: the business roughly tripled in 2 years; the recent slowdown
    looks like a one-off dip rather than a structural problem, since newer
    customers are more loyal, not less.

FREE SHIPPING
    Problem: at first glance the free-shipping threshold looks like it costs
    a lot of value in the "drop" range.
    Plan: understand why that drop happens.
    Data: compare profit in other ranges against the "drop" range.
    Analysis: the drop is actually GOOD for the company, because free-shipping
    status makes customers spend more. I also checked whether converting
    customers from paid to free shipping is worthwhile, and it is.
    Conclusion: free shipping drives a substantial increase in sales/profit,
    and converting customers does too. We should keep incentivising free
    shipping and try to move more paid-shipping customers to it.
    Idea: an experiment with one group offered free shipping at a lower order
    threshold than usual, one group with 50% off shipping at a lower bracket,
    and a control group, to see if this creates more high-value customers.
    To quantify for the deck: profit impact if we convert 20% of paid-shipping
    customers to free shipping.

HIGH-VALUE CUSTOMERS
    Problem: understand who our best customers are and how valuable they are.
    Plan: analyse the top 10% - where they come from, how much they spend -
    and compare them to the average customer.
    Data: which countries have a high HV %, how much HV customers are worth,
    and GDP vs HV rate.
    Analysis: DE, US and FR have more HV than regular customers. HV customers
    are ~3.2x more valuable than non-HV. Countries like DK, FI, SE, NL, NO
    have high GDP but a much lower HV rate.
    Conclusion: HV customers are very valuable and worth retaining.
    The US is a large outlier on GDP per capita; Norway is the opposite.
    The underperforming countries are in northern Europe, so a targeted
    campaign there to win HV customers makes sense.
    To quantify for the deck: lost profit vs if these countries matched their
    GDP-expected HV rate - profit at GDP-expected rate, and at 20% above.

REPEAT CUSTOMERS
    Problem: only 14.2% of customers place a second order.
    Plan: confirm this in the data and see how a customer evolves over time.
    Analysis: lifetime value grows ~50% per additional order, driven by
    higher frequency rather than bigger baskets.
    Conclusion: don't push for bigger baskets; push for the next purchase,
    e.g. a next-buy incentive. Customers are compounding ~1.4x more valuable
    per order at ~2.2x the frequency over a 12-month average, so a loyalty
    campaign could work.
    To quantify for the deck: impact if we convert 25% of non-loyal customers
    to loyal.

COUNTRY PROFITABILITY
    Problem: Q2 ranked countries by HV conversion rate but never checked
    whether margin/profit per order actually varies by country, or whether
    the shipping cost structure is eating profit in some markets.
    Plan: build country-level profit per order and margin, and check against
    shipping fee as a % of average order value.
    Analysis: margin swings from 54.4% (US) to 70.9% (DE) among top markets,
    far more than the order-volume ranking suggests. Shipping as % of AOV
    explains profit per order (corr -0.73) but NOT margin (corr 0.08),
    because Profit = Total_amount * Profit_margin exactly in the data, so
    margin is a flat rate not reduced by shipping cost directly.
    Conclusion: GB is the weak spot - lowest free-shipping rate (48.6% vs
    71-74% for DE/SE, which ties back to Q1) AND a separate margin gap to DE
    worth ~4.6M / +22% profit if closed. US/CA are a different issue: highest
    AOV but lowest margin despite the lowest shipping-cost share, so not a
    shipping fix - looks like a product-mix / cost-structure question.
    To quantify for the deck: the 4.6M GB margin-gap figure. Two separate
    playbooks are needed per market, not one universal fix.
